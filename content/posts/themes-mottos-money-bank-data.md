# Themes & Mottos — Money Is a File Format, Be the Bank, Own Your Data

There is a moment in every long fight where the language collapses. The arguments get louder, the slogans get louder, the experts get more credentialed, and the actual thing that needs to be said gets smaller and harder to find, because the people with the microphones are too busy using the words to mean themselves.

This is that moment. The microphone is somewhere else. The thing that needs to be said is small.

It is five sentences.

**The internet eats everything.**

**The things you may not have seen, you may not have seen because of greed and scams.**

**These battles are institutional.**

**Money is a file format.**

**Be the bank. Own your data.**

That is the whole doctrine. The rest of this piece is the unpacking, and the unpacking is for the people who have done some version of the work already and need the words to be put somewhere they can be carried.

If you arrived here from a product page, that is the right place to start. If you arrived here from a decade of carrying, the words are probably not new to you. They are just the names for things you have been holding.

Either way — read it slowly. The sentences are short on purpose.

---

## I. The internet eats everything

There is a sentence people used to say about the printing press. The printing press ate the monastery. It ate the scriptorium. It ate the cathedral's monopoly on who got to read and who got to be read to. It ate a whole economy of scribes, and a whole class of people whose authority came from controlling the books. It did not eat them out of malice. It ate them because the new thing was cheaper, faster, and reachable by anyone with a hand and a day.

The internet is doing the same thing. It is eating the newsroom, the bank branch, the record store, the classified ad, the photograph, the map, the letter, the diary, the address book, the alarm clock, the calendar, the encyclopedia, the school, the stockbroker, the auction house, the lawyer's filing cabinet, the doctor's office, the voter's roll, the tax return, the passport office, the friend group, the family group, the date, the breakup, the meeting, the sermon, the AA meeting, the prayer, the bequest, the will, the video rental store, the music store, the bookstore, the library card.

It eats them.

The eating is not the problem. The eating was always going to happen. The printing press ate the monastery, and the people who survived the eating built a new world on top of the press. The eating is the new substrate. The problem is who is doing the eating, and what they are doing with the meal, and whether the people who used to own the things being eaten have any say in what gets built on top of the place where those things used to be.

That is the part the cycle does not want to talk about. The cycle is too busy building the new thing on top of the old thing's bones to notice that the bones belonged to someone. So the cycle says: this is the future, you are going to love it, the future is here. The cycle does not say: here is the cost. Here is who paid it. Here is what the cost is now.

We are going to say it.

The internet ate the bank. The internet ate the newsroom. The internet ate the school. The internet ate the photo album. The internet ate the address book. The internet ate the diary. The internet ate the way you find a job and the way you find a date and the way you find a friend and the way you find a ride and the way you find a place to sleep and the way you find out what is happening in the country you live in and the way you find out whether the country you live in is telling the truth about itself.

The internet did not eat these things because it is evil. The internet ate these things because it was cheaper, faster, and reachable by more people than the things that came before. That is what new technology does. That is what the press did. That is what the engine did. That is what electricity did. The eating is the substrate.

The question is what gets built on the substrate. And that question is the one the cycle is losing the right to answer alone, because the cycle has spent thirty years building the substrate in a way that the cycle alone gets to decide what is for.

The internet eats everything. What gets built on top of what it ate is the fight. The fight is not a fight against the internet. The fight is for the internet. The fight is for the part of the internet that the cycle has been keeping for itself.

That is the first sentence.

## II. The things you may not have seen, you may not have seen because of greed and scams

There is a thing that happens to people who have been doing their own work for a long time. You start to notice that you and the people you trust are looking at a different world than the people on the screen. You notice that the headlines that get pushed at you do not match the experience you are having. You notice that the news that gets covered is the news that serves the advertisers, the donors, the campaigns, the platforms, the institutional players whose interests are downstream of the coverage. You notice that the things you actually care about — the things happening on the ground in your town, in your block, in your industry, in your field, in the field of people you have been doing the work with — are not on the screen. They are not on the screen because they are not profitable to put on the screen. They are not on the screen because the people who would put them on the screen cannot afford to put them on the screen. They are not on the screen because the screen is paid for by the people who would rather the things you care about stay off it.

You start to suspect that the things you may not have seen, you may not have seen for a reason.

You are right.

The reason is not always a conspiracy. The reason is sometimes a conspiracy. The reason is more often a quiet, structural fact about how the system is built. The system is built by people whose job is to extract from you — your attention, your data, your time, your money, your consent, your belief that the system is the only system — and the way you get extracted from is by being shown a small slice of the world, on a schedule, in a tone, and by people who benefit from your being shown that slice.

Greed is the part you can see. Scams are the part you can see. Greed and scams are loud, and they are covered, and the cycle makes sure you can see them, because the cycle needs you to think the problem is greed and scams. The cycle needs you to think the problem is a few bad actors. The cycle needs you to think the problem is fixable by voting for the right person, or installing the right app, or buying the right subscription, or believing the right expert, or trusting the right institution.

The problem is not a few bad actors. The problem is the system that produces the bad actors. The system produces the bad actors by paying the bad actors to be the bad actors, and by paying the experts to tell you the bad actors are the problem, and by paying the news to cover the bad actors, and by paying the platforms to amplify the bad actors, and by paying the regulators to regulate the bad actors in ways that do not threaten the system that produced the bad actors.

That is the part that is hard to see. That is the part you may not have seen.

The things you may not have seen, you may not have seen because the people who would have shown them to you are being paid not to. You may not have seen them because the people who would have shown them to you were outcompeted, defunded, discredited, bought, or driven out. You may not have seen them because the people who would have shown them to you are running a small operation on a threadbare budget, and the people on the other side are running a media company. You may not have seen them because the people on the other side are running the platform the small operation is on, and the platform is being paid to make sure the small operation does not get the reach the operation on the other side gets.

You may not have seen them because they were real, and the people who are paid to show you things do not get paid to show you real things. They get paid to show you the things that keep the system that pays them running.

This is not a paranoid reading. This is a structural reading. The system is built to produce this. The system is built so that the people who would tell you the truth about the system are outcompeted, defunded, and discredited, and the people who would tell you a comforting story about the system are amplified, funded, and credentialed. The system is built so that you are shown a small slice of the world by people who benefit from you being shown that slice, and the rest of the world is being kept off the screen by people who benefit from it being kept off the screen.

The things you may not have seen, you may not have seen for a reason. The reason is the system. The system is not a conspiracy. The system is a pattern. The pattern is the reason.

That is the second sentence.

## III. These battles are institutional

This is the one most people do not want to hear.

The people who do not want to hear it are the people who have spent a long time working on the assumption that the problem is one bad actor, one bad law, one bad institution, one bad election, one bad year. The people who do not want to hear it are the people who have spent a long time believing that the work is to find the right person, put the right person in the right office, and the rest will take care of itself.

That work is not nothing. That work has its place. Voting matters. Organizing matters. Holding the line on a single bad law matters. Building a single good institution matters. Putting a single good person in a single bad office matters.

That is not the fight.

The fight is institutional. The fight is about the institutions. The fight is about the structures that decide which people get to run which offices, which laws get to be written, which stories get to be covered, which experts get to be credentialed, which platforms get to decide which speech is allowed, which money gets to be moved, which data gets to be collected, which communities get to be funded, and which communities get to be starved.

The fight is about the institutions because the institutions are the things that outlast the people. The institutions are the things that decide who gets to be the next person in the office, and what the person in the office is allowed to do, and what the people under the person in the office are paid to do, and what the people outside the person in the office are allowed to know about what the people inside the person in the office are doing.

The institutions are the layer most people are not looking at. The institutions are the layer most people are not even taught to look at. The institutions are the layer that, when you do look at it, makes most of the other layers make sense.

A bad election is not just a bad election. A bad election is the symptom of an institution that has been captured so that bad elections keep happening, and so that the people who would have to fix the institution are produced by the institution. A bad law is not just a bad law. A bad law is the output of an institution that has been captured so that bad laws keep getting written, and so that the people who would have to fix the law are the people who wrote the bad law. A bad platform is not just a bad platform. A bad platform is the output of an institution that has been captured so that bad platforms keep getting built, and so that the people who would have to fix the platform are the people who profit from the platform.

You cannot win this fight at the level of the bad actor. You cannot win it at the level of the bad election. You cannot win it at the level of the bad law. You can only win it at the level of the institution.

That is the third sentence. And the third sentence is the one the people who have been doing the work longest already know, even if they have not said it out loud.

## IV. Money is a file format

For most of human history, money was a thing you held.

It was a coin. It was a note. It was a bead. It was a shell. It was a cow. It was a bushel of grain. It was a claim on a bushel of grain held by a temple, or a granary, or a guild, or a king. The thing that was held had weight, or it had a smell, or it had a shape. The thing that was held was physical, and the holding was physical, and the trust was in the holding.

That is not what money is anymore.

Money today is a file. A row in a database, somewhere, that says a number is associated with a name, or with an address, or with an account, or with a hash. The file is updated when a payment is made. The file is read when a payment is checked. The file is closed when the institution running the database decides the file is closed. The file is seized when the institution running the database, or the institution the institution is accountable to, decides the file is to be seized. The file is invisible to you most of the time, and the file is only visible to the people running the institution running the database.

That is the whole shape of the system.

The system is a small number of institutions running a small number of databases, and the databases say what money is, and the institutions say who can move the money, and the institutions say who can see the money, and the institutions say who can be cut off from the money, and the institutions decide which of their customers get charged what fee, and which of their merchants get settled on what schedule, and which of their citizens get to be debanked, and which of their adversaries get to be sanctioned, and which of their governments get to be cut off from the rails.

The money is not yours. The money was never yours. The money was always the institution's record of what it owes you, in the form the institution chose, on the schedule the institution chose, with the dependencies the institution chose.

Until the file got a different shape.

A file is a file. A database row is a database row. A file can be copied. A database row can be read by anyone who can read the database. A file can be moved. A database row can be moved by anyone who has the key. A file can be checked. A database row can be checked by anyone running a node.

The file got a different shape when the file stopped being held by a small number of institutions and started being held by a network.

That is what money is now. Money is a file on a network. Money is a file that the network agrees is the file. Money is a file that anyone running a node can verify, and that no one running a node can change without the network agreeing the file is to be changed.

Money is a file format. The file format is open. The network is open. The verification is open. The fact that the file is yours is open, in the sense that the file is on a network you can run a node on, and the file's history is something you can check, and the file's movement is something you can verify, and the file's scarcity is something the network enforces, not the institution.

That is what money is now. Money is a file. The file is yours. The file is on a network. The network is not a bank. The network is not a state. The network is the file.

That is the fourth sentence.

## V. Be the bank

The bank is the institution that runs the database. The bank is the institution that decides who can see the database, and who can move money in the database, and who gets cut off from the database, and which of its customers get charged what fee, and which of its merchants get settled on what schedule, and which of its citizens get to be debanked.

The bank is also the institution that does not always act in your interest. The bank is the institution that has a long history of not always acting in your interest. The bank is the institution that has, in many places, in many decades, in many currencies, decided that the people who are not profitable to bank are not bankable. The bank is the institution that has decided that the people who are politically inconvenient are not bankable. The bank is the institution that has decided that the people who are running a charity, or a community, or a mutual aid network, or a journalism outfit, or a small business in a regulated industry, or a researcher in a disfavored field, or a citizen in a disfavored country, are not bankable.

The bank has the power to do this because the bank runs the database. The database is the money. The bank runs the money. The bank runs who can have money, and how, and on what terms, and for how long.

The bank is also the institution that is the most profitable target for the people who would rather not have a free press, or a free opposition, or a free civic life, or a free way to move value across a border. The bank is the choke point. The bank is the place where the state, the criminal, the corporation, and the platform all meet to decide who gets to be in the system and who gets to be out.

The bank is also the institution that, for most of us, holds the file that says we have what we have. The bank is also the institution that decides whether the file is to be there tomorrow, and the day after, and the day after that.

Be the bank.

Not in the sense of being a bank. Not in the sense of running the kind of institution that ran the kind of database that produced the kind of file the cycle used to extract from. Not in the sense of becoming a new institution that decides who gets to be in the system.

Be the bank in the sense of running your own node. Be the bank in the sense of holding your own key. Be the bank in the sense of not asking anyone's permission to hold what you hold, or to move what you move, or to send what you send, or to receive what you receive.

Be the bank in the sense of being the place where the file is. Be the bank in the sense of being the place where the file is verified. Be the bank in the sense of being the place where the file's history is checked.

Be the bank in the sense of being the institution that the institution has to ask permission from, instead of the other way around.

That is the fifth sentence. The fifth sentence is the one the bank does not want you to say out loud. The bank does not want you to say it out loud because the sentence is the one that ends the bank's monopoly on the file. The bank's monopoly on the file is the bank's monopoly on the world. The bank's monopoly on the world is the thing the cycle is built on top of.

Be the bank. The world is on the other side of the sentence.

## VI. Own your data

The last sentence is the one most people have heard before. Most people have heard the sentence in the form of a tip. Use a VPN. Install a blocker. Read the terms of service. Cover the camera. Set the phone down. Most people have heard the sentence in the form of a setting. Toggle the privacy setting. Opt out of the cookies. Decline the marketing email. Most people have heard the sentence in the form of a market. Buy the subscription. Pay the company. Trust the brand.

None of those are the sentence.

The sentence is older than the tip and the setting and the market. The sentence is a posture. The sentence is the posture of a person who has decided that the things that come out of them — the things they think, the things they read, the things they write, the things they buy, the things they say to the people they love, the things they say to the people they are trying to love, the things they say to the people they are trying to figure out, the things they say to themselves — belong to them. The things that come out of them are not the property of the system that records the things that come out of them. The things that come out of them are not the property of the platform that hosts the recording. The things that come out of them are not the property of the people who pay the platform to host the recording.

The things that come out of them are the things that come out of them. The things that come out of them are the work of a life. The work of a life is not the property of the system that records the life. The work of a life is the property of the life.

That is the posture. The posture is older than the internet. The posture is older than the printing press. The posture is older than the bank. The posture is the posture of a person who has decided that the work of a life is the work of the life, and not the work of the institution that records the life.

The internet made the posture urgent. The internet made the posture difficult. The internet made the posture a thing that has to be practiced, every day, with tools, with discipline, with the kind of attention most people do not have the time or the training to bring to the work of a Tuesday afternoon.

Own your data is not a tip. Own your data is not a setting. Own your data is a posture, and the posture is non-negotiable, and the posture is the only posture that lets a person be the person the person is, instead of the person the system that records the person says the person is.

That is the sixth sentence. The sixth sentence is the same sentence as the first five. The first five sentences are about the world. The sixth sentence is about the person. The world and the person are the same doctrine. The doctrine is small. The doctrine is five sentences long. The doctrine is a posture. The doctrine is held in common by the people who refuse to be the resource.

That is the whole thing.

## VII. The doctrine

Here are the six sentences, together, so they can be carried.

The internet eats everything.

The things you may not have seen, you may not have seen because of greed and scams.

These battles are institutional.

Money is a file format.

Be the bank. Own your data.

That is the doctrine. The doctrine is not a manifesto. The doctrine is a set of postures. The postures are not for the people who have already arrived. The postures are for the people who are arriving, in the dark, in the middle of the work, in the middle of the carrying, who need the words to be put somewhere they can be carried.

If you arrived here from a product page, that is the right place to start.

If you arrived here from a decade of carrying, the words are not new. The words are the names of things you have been holding.

The carrying does not get lighter. The carrying gets a shape. The shape is the doctrine. The doctrine is six sentences long. The sentences are short on purpose.

The work is the work. The thread is still here.
